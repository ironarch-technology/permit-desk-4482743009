# Read this first

This repository is what we'll talk about in your interview. We built it for that conversation.
You don't have to hand anything in, and you don't have to get it running.

## What you're looking at

Permit Desk is the service behind a building permit portal for the City of Mountport. Mountport
is made up. Someone applies for a permit, and Permit Desk moves the application through zoning,
plan review, corrections, fees and payment until a permit is issued. Other city systems make the
actual decisions. Permit Desk coordinates them and keeps the record of what happened.

It's a complete system that runs, and it's written the way a team writes when there's a deadline.
We haven't tidied it or left notes in it for you.

## What to do before we meet

Read it. Give it an hour or two. You don't need to read every file.

You can run it if you like. The README explains how. It isn't required, though some of what this
system does is easier to see when an application is moving through it.

Then come with a view. We'd like to hear:

- what you noticed
- what you'd fix first, and why that one ahead of the others
- what you'd want to know before this went live to the public
- what you'd leave alone

We do mean the last one. Deciding what not to touch is part of the work.

## AI tools

Use them if you normally would. Most people do, and it isn't a mark against you.

It will come up when we talk. We'll ask what you used, what you kept from it and what you threw
away. That's a real question and there's no wrong answer hiding behind it. Working well with
these tools is part of the job.

A tool will find plenty in this codebase, and it'll rank what it finds and write you a plan if
you ask. What it can't do is defend that ranking when we push on it. It doesn't know what this
team can afford to do this month, and it doesn't know what would change your mind. So bring an
order you'd argue for yourself.

## The session

It's an hour, with two of us. About half of that is spent on this code.

We'll open by asking what would stop you putting this live. After that we'll look at a few places
in the code together and ask what you make of them, and then you choose where we go. We'll end
on what your first week with this codebase would look like.

There's no code to write in the session and no take-home afterwards.

## A few things that are true

We aren't counting findings. A handful of things you can rank and argue for is worth more to us
than a long list.

"I don't know, but here's how I'd find out" is a good answer. We'd much rather hear that than a
guess.

There's no trick here and nothing you have to catch to do well. We want to see how you think
about a codebase you didn't write.

## Questions

If anything here is unclear, ask us before the session. Asking doesn't count against you.
